🌐 Multiplayer (up to 2v2)

On champion select choose ⚡ Quick Match (auto-joins any open game — a waiting lobby or a running match with a free seat — or hosts one that starts vs NPC in 25s), Host Room (you get a 4-letter room code) or Join Room (enter the host's code), then pick a champion. The lobby lists everyone; the host presses START. Players can also join mid-game — they take over the AI hero with their own champion, or drop in as a 3rd/4th hero (max 2v2). When joining you choose a side: 🤝 Co-op (fight beside the host — the AI keeps the enemy hero), ⚔ Versus (enemy team), or 🎲 Any (auto-balance).

  • Same network: friends just open the address shown in the lobby (http://<host-ip>:8000) in their browser.
  • Signaling: uses the free PeerJS cloud by default; run start_peer_server.bat for a fully local LAN server (auto-detected).
  • Economy online: each hero has their own gold purse (kills, bosses, towers); the shop deducts from it. Town halls and lane armies are shared per side; the host additionally runs the RTS economy.
  • Disconnects: if a red player leaves, the AI takes their hero back over.
  • 🏆 Tournaments: the host can stake the match — entry is 1 💎CT (paid joiners get a 💎 badge; unpaid players may help but earn nothing). Winners get their entry back plus 50% of the losing/forfeited fees, split. Dropping out forfeits your fee.

💎 CT (Carat) & EF HUNT — the PVE grind mode

CT is the cross-mode currency (you start with 2). Stake it in MOBA tournaments — and earn it in EF HUNT (pve.html), a separate PVE survival game in the same folder: pick from 3 daily-rotating hunters, fight scaling monster waves, tame hatchlings from eggs, gather wood/gold, build towers/walls/heal totems, survive mini-bosses every 30 minutes and slay the final boss at the 4-hour mark to clear the map. But the name carries the story's core twist — the Hunt is reversed: once Beyond the Twilight Veil carries you across the Rift into the Underworld, you are no longer the hunter but the hunted, stalked through the dark and surviving only by masking your scent. EF HUNT names the prey you become, not the prey you chase.

  • Earnings: CT checkpoints at 1h / 2.5h / map-clear (20/30/50% of the difficulty payout). Difficulty 1 pays just 3 CT for the full 4 hours — difficulty 9 ("hell") pays 30.
  • Permanent gear: 6 upgrade lines (attack, HP, speed, taming, harvest, pet damage), 3 tiers each, bought with CT. Gear carries over between runs — but monsters scale up with your gear score, so power has a price.
  • Hardcore: die without a 🧪 revive potion (2 CT) and you lose ALL gear and difficulty progress — only your CT balance survives. Clear the map and difficulty rises +1 (max 9); the run-over screen also names the next campaign chapter you just unlocked (max Episode 7).
  • Pacing: one run per 8 hours. Saving CT makes you richer; spending it makes deeper difficulties reachable. Choose wisely.
  • Humanity ⇄ Corruption (story mode): in Beyond the Twilight Veil a Corruption gauge sits under your mana — but only once you've crossed the Rift. The warm human world (Episodes 1–2) is carefree: no gauge, no hunt, no survival pressure. The instant you cross into the Underworld (Episode 3) the Humanity bar appears, with a one-time warning that you are now the hunted — a new survival mode begins. From there on, casting skills (especially your ultimate) and lingering in the Underworld raise it — and the deeper the realm, the faster the dark erodes you; resting in the carnival hub lowers it. Cross the Power Threshold and your shadow power awakens — every skill hits harder the deeper the shadow runs (scaling up to +60% skill damage as you near the brink), and your ultimate also gains a haste-frenzy; max it past the Critical Limit and you are Consumed by the Veil — a death. Keep your Humanity in the light. As your Corruption climbs, the 🕷 Whisper (the dark's own voice) murmurs a taunt at each new depth you reach — flavour that marks how far you've slipped; your 📜 Journal keeps a 🕷 The Whisper record of how many of its depths you've heard this descent and its last words to you. Choice moments: once per run, as each chapter begins, the story pauses to offer a dark/light choice — the dark option buys power but feeds the Veil (raises Corruption), the light option holds to your humanity (lowers it). Your pick flavours the next line of dialogue and nudges your gauge, so every descent is a fresh negotiation with yourself. Your choices this run are tallied into 🌗 Your Path in the 📜 Journal — and, once you've made your first choice, surfaced live in the top-bar status indicator (🌑 shadow-leaning, 🕯 light-leaning, ☯ balanced) so you can read your drift mid-run — lean dark and you read as Shadow-Touched or Veil-Bound, hold to the light and you stay Pure of Heart; the tally resets each run. And at the very end, your path is spoken to: when you clear the final boss, the ending closes with one extra narrator line keyed to the choices you made this descent — the Veil knowing your name all along if you went dark, or finding nothing it could take if you held to the light.
  • 🍞 Anchors of Humanity (human food): the carnival hub is your safest balm, but once you're deep in the Underworld (Episode 3+) relief is scattered on the ground — loaves of human food, each lit by a soft warm glow. Walk over one to eat it and pull your Corruption gauge back toward Humanity. Unlike gold and gear, food is not drawn to you — you must go to it, so each loaf pings the minimap as a warm-amber ✚ (it always shows, even through unexplored fog) — a low-Humanity hunter can always see where relief is. This is Yume's Episode-6 bargain made real: eating masks your scent for a breath — the Underworld's hunters lose track of you and break off the chase, a brief respite in the deep. A 🍞 MASKED countdown appears in the top-bar status indicator while it lasts, so you know how long the window is open. It won't fool a boss, and any creature you've already struck stays on you; when the scent returns the hunt resumes. (Food appears only in the Underworld, and not at all while you are Consumed by the Veil.)
  • Chapters & cutscenes: the campaign runs across seven episodes (📖 Chapters on the title screen), each opening with a first-person story cutscene. Episode 2 "Masquerade" introduces the fox-masked Yui; Episode 3 "The Rite of Blades" is the blood-pact at the portal, where the living Blood Scimitar brands you — your first shadow power awakens and, ever after, every run begins with a faint corruption residue. From the Rift down, the descent crosses the three Realms of Desire that re-frame the Underworld zones — the Luring Garden (Ep4 — desire as the wonder that wants to keep you), the Hungering Dance (Ep5 — desire as consumption, the crowd that eats you alive) and the Hollow Wastes (Ep6 — desire as the price come due). Episode 4 "Garden of Whispers" shrinks you into a dream-garden where the river of visions shows a chilling foreshadow of the betrayal to come. Episode 5 "Dance Macabre" turns a beautiful dance into the Ballroom of Nightmares: light the colour-hungry blooms to open a path while a dancer-swarm hunts you, and watch the dark drag Ayume down toward the throne — a "capture" that may not be one. Episode 6 "Shadows of the Self" strands you in the Hollow Wastes, where the human girl Yume gives you a compass to the Castle of Shadows and human bread: eat human food to pull your Corruption back toward Humanity and, for a breath, mask your scent so the hunt loses you — though the quiet is brief, no Warden is fooled, and leaning back toward your humanity dims the shadow-power the Veil lends: power or peace, never both. Episode 7 "Kage no Mamoru" storms the Castle of Shadows (Yomi no Tō): a boss gauntlet against the shadow-lord Kage no Mamoru, where the Corruption gauge becomes the whole fight — stay below the Power Threshold and your blade barely scratches him as he mends in the dark, so you must embrace the shadow to wound him, yet cross the Critical Limit and the Veil consumes you mid-fight. The throne waits one door beyond. Every cutscene is voiced — each speaker has their own tonal bark as they talk (bright for Yui, low and grinding for Kage no Mamoru, an eager woof for Barkindle), with soft stings as a scene opens and closes and a dark/light chime when you take a choice. The story's pivotal beats — the 🕷 Whisper reaching a new Corruption depth, the instant Ayume reveals herself, and the alignment line that closes your ending — land on a deeper reveal sting, a low dread-stab that tells your ear something just turned — paired with a blood-red flash across the screen and a shudder of the dialogue box, so the moment still lands with the sound off. Cutscenes play from the keyboard too: Space or Enter advances a line, Esc skips the scene, and when a dark/light choice is on screen 1 takes the darker option and 2 the lighter — so the whole campaign is replayable without the mouse. So the controls are never a secret, a small hint line under every dialogue box spells them out (▶ click / Space · Esc ⏭), and the dark/light choice buttons are numbered 1./2. to match their keys. Replaying a chapter you've already watched? A ⏭ skip tab on every dialogue box (or Esc) jumps to the end of the scene (it still hands off to whatever the scene was about to do) — but it always stops at a dark/light choice, so your path is never skipped for you. The 🔊 toggle mutes the audio.
  • The betrayal & the true final boss: felling Kage no Mamoru does not end the story — it opens the throne. Ayume orchestrated the whole descent: she found The Scimitar's Secrets and needed a fated partner to carry the blade and ripen on the way down. At the throne she absorbs Yui into the onyx stone and becomes the true final boss — Ayume herself. Defeat her and your ending is chosen by the difficulty you cleared (1 = the bleakest, 9 = the Holy Grail, the only ending where Yui is redeemed — clearing it plays the full epilogue in the moment you earn it, and the ▶ Memories viewer replays it after). Clear a chapter to unlock the next. Your 📜 Journal tracks an 🎭 Endings Witnessed roll (n / 9) — every ending you've reached and your furthest clear, each remembered with a one-line first-person echo of how it ended for you (sealed rungs stay spoiler-free) — so you can see which rungs of the difficulty ladder are still left to earn.
  • 📖 The Story So Far (in-run chapter recap): once you're in a hunt the chapter objective is easy to forget — so your 📜 Journal now opens with The Story So Far: the episode you're playing, its setting and your party's stance, your 🎯 objective for this descent, and a ladder of all seven chapters marking which you've cleared (✔), where you are (▸) and what's still sealed (🔒). And the moment a descent begins — once the opening cutscene and your dark/light choice are past — that 🎯 objective also flashes up as an on-screen banner, so you're reminded what you came to do.
  • 📜 Memories of the Veil (lore codex): living the story reclaims lost memories into your 📜 Journal — one for each chapter you play (the carnival night, the fox in the grove, the living scimitar, the river of visions, the staged scream, Yume's warning, the onyx throne), plus two hidden OVA fragments: 📖 The Bookshop (Ayume's origin) is recovered when the throne opens at the betrayal, and ✒ The First Page (the truth) is recovered on a difficulty-6 clear — the same moment the ▶ Memories OVA viewer unlocks on the title (your first difficulty-6 clear also pays a one-time 💎 +2 CT for unsealing the truth). Collect all nine to complete the record. The memories are pure lore (no combat effect) and survive death like your story progress.
  • Barkindle (your starter pet) is never lost for good: if your faithful companion Barkindle falls in the Underworld, he isn't gone — return to Emberhollow and press B near the Kennel (the doghouse, with its food bowl) to revive him for gold. The cost rises with your difficulty, and he comes back at full strength (scaled to your difficulty, level and Taming tier). Pet death never feels permanent for the signature companion. And in a boss fight your pets can't read the red-ring slam telegraph the way you can, so a slam catches them — but they take only half its damage (you take it full), enough to keep the pack alive at your side for a hit or two instead of being wiped every cast.
  • Commanding a pet (MOBA-style): pets fight and gather on their own — and when no enemy is right beside a pet, it now piles onto whatever you're attacking rather than standing idle, so the pack fights alongside you and helps run down the ranged stragglers that try to kite you in the deeper realms. You can also give one a direct order — left-click a pet to select it (a blue ring appears), then right-click an enemy to send it to attack, right-click the ground to move it, or press S to stop / H to hold position. It's a single order: after one command, control returns to your hero automatically and the pet carries out the order before resuming its own behaviour.
  • Companions: Ayume and Yui stand beside you as party members — Ayume accompanies you through the monster-free Carnival, and Yui joins as an allied unit who hunts monsters with you once the descent begins (from the Garden of Whispers onward). Each appears in their true form — Ayume wears Leah's model, Yui wears Irene's. They are loyal… for now. Late in the tale a companion can turn against you, joining the enemy and hunting you instead — the gentlest face among them was the Veil's own.
  • The four Sin-Realms & their Realm-lords: beyond the carnival the Veil is sculpted from human desire into four named realms, each a 🗺 zone with its own boss. Together these four are the second of the three Realms of Desire — the Hungering Dance: desire as consumption, the crowd that eats you alive. Valley of Midas (Greed) — streets of living gold where the covetous gild themselves solid — ruled by Aurelian, the Gilded King, who rains molten gold and ENRAGES at low HP. Enamora (Lust) — a garden of perpetual twilight, sweet with rot — ruled by Seraphel, the Beguiling Rose, who rends the earth with grasping thorns. Beyond them all stands the Castle of Shadows (Yomi no Tō). Realms open one at a time, in order: fell a realm's Warden and its key unseals the next, so a far chapter (e.g. the Ep7 Castle / Abyssal Vault) stays locked until you've cleared the realms before it — and if you pick a still-sealed realm the game drops you back in the Wilds and tells you the exact Warden and realm to clear next. Each realm's lore reads on the 🗺 Choose Zone screen, where the one realm you can open right now is flagged ▶ NEXT (amber) with the Warden and open realm that unseal it. The screen now reads as a climb at a glance: realms you've cleared show ✔ (green — Warden felled, key taken), your current rung shows ◆ Frontier (fell its Warden to unseal the next), the openable one shows ▶ NEXT, and the rest stay 🔒 sealed.

Your first reading is a full prophecy that warns of the allure of power (the Corruption gauge) and quietly seeds the betrayal — including a third card she covers before you can see it.   • Fog of war (exploring): every hunt the world starts shrouded. The minimap and the 🗺 world map (M) reveal only the ground you've actually walked — a lit bubble follows you and leaves an explored trail. Resource nodes, dropped loot and lurking enemies stay hidden in the fog until you reach their part of the map, rewarding players who roam the far edges. The fog clears and resets with each new run.
  • Objective compass (🧭): a gold needle on the minimap always points toward the current chapter's goal — Yume's enchanted compass (Ep6), generalised to every episode. Ep1 points home to the carnival; later chapters point to that chapter's realm; Ep7 holds dead-steady on the Castle of Shadows. The needle rests once you reach the destination zone.
  • Reading the enemy (threat markers): a coloured con marker floats over every monster — green easy, white even, orange ! tough, red ☠ deadly, ??? flee — so you can size up the danger before you commit. A small ⚠ THREAT legend sits just above the minimap (bottom-right), keeping the colour scale on screen at all times. Monsters also have moods: most chase, a few ignore you until struck, some hound you back to town, clever ones break off when losing.
  • World pickups (finds on the road): each hunt scatters generous finds across the Wilds — gold piles, wood bundles and dropped gear — every one tagged with a faint golden glint visible from afar. Approach a find and it is vacuumed straight to you (no clicking). The best caches, and double-value gold, lie out on the far edges of the map, so roaming pays — but the roaming packs you meet there hit harder and soak more the further you stray from Emberhollow (gentle by the town, up to ~50% tougher at the rim), so the richest ground is also the most dangerous.
  • Hidden caches (gated corners): three treasure caches are sealed in the hardest-to-reach spots each hunt — atop the two high mesa crowns (the slow climb gates them) and on a far map edge — each marked by a tall amber pillar of light. A cache is not vacuumed; you must climb or trek all the way to it. Opening one yields a guaranteed Rare-or-better weapon/armor, bonus gold and a gem — the payoff for reaching the edges. Rare reclaims now carry their own sound: a triumphant chime for a cracked cache, a warm tone for a human-food anchor, and an ethereal note when a memory is recovered (🔊 toggles audio).

🚪 Getting Started

Run start_game.bat (serves the game at http://localhost:8000 — required so 3D models and the monster roster can load), then open the game in your browser. You'll land on the login screen: enter a summoner name (saved in your browser) and press Enter the Rift. Your name and all progression — including hero mastery — live in browser localStorage; nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Win / Lose: destroy the enemy core to win. You only lose when all of your bases fall — your hero dying just means a respawn timer.
Match length: tuned for 10–20 minutes (modern fast-MOBA pacing). Minion waves grow +8% per minute, and at 15:00 OVERTIME begins: structures take 35% extra damage, so stalemates break fast. Respawn timers also lengthen as the game ages.

🐲 Champions & Mastery

Every champion is a real monster from the Ethermon roster with one or two elemental types. Its primary type decides its Q/W/E skill kit. Champions come from evolution chains, so your hero can evolve into stronger forms mid-match.

★ Hero Mastery

The more you play a hero, the marginally better you get with it — like a personal rune page that grows with practice:

  • You earn 1 mastery point per 15 seconds played on a hero, plus a bonus for finishing a match (+10 win / +4 loss).
  • Mastery level = capped curve up to Lv 10 (Lv 10 needs 1,000 points).
  • Each level grants +0.5% HP and attack on that hero — hard-capped at +5% so it stays flavor, never pay-to-win-by-grinding.
  • Mastery is shown on every champion card and stored in your browser (localStorage).

✨ Skills

Skills use modern aim-then-cast targeting: press the key, see a live preview (ring for area, line for direction), click to cast. Clicking near the enemy hero snaps the cast onto them. Each of the 17 elements maps to a kit built from six archetypes:

ArchetypeWhat it does
NovaLobbed blast that erupts for area damage at the cursor
LinePiercing shot in a straight line
RingGround slam around you — damages and slows
BuffAttack-speed surge for a few seconds
DashQuick reposition toward the cursor
BlinkInstant teleport to the target point

Skills cost mana and scale with hero level (XP from creeps, kills, structures and passive trickle).

💰 Economy

Two resources: gold and wood. Your hero earns gold from kills; peons (pet monsters) gather both — StarCraft style. Select a peon, right-click a tree or gold vein, and it hauls loads back to the nearest base and keeps going. Trees are solid until chopped.

  • Killing enemies and wilds can drop eggs — walk your hero or a pet over one to hatch a free peon.
  • Gold also gates the Item Shop, training units, buildings and evolution — spend wisely.
  • Caps: 32 peons, 32 soldiers.

🏰 Building

Glowing rings on the map are build slots. Click one to build a Tower (auto-attacks) or an Expansion Base (up to 3 bases total). Expansion slots near gold are guarded by wild camps — clear the guards to unlock them. Click a base or your town hall for its menu: upgrade defense, train peons / footmen / archers, and evolve your hero.

👑 Boss Zones & Legendary Loot

Two boss lairs sit in the previously-unused jungle quadrants (purple rings on the map and minimap) — and they hold the high ground: each lair crowns a hill, and climbing or descending the ramps slows all units by up to ~45% (Dota-style high-ground pressure, but as a movement cost). Pale circles on the minimap mark the hills. Each lair holds a powerful boss (2,400 HP) flanked by elite guards:

LairBoss dropStats
North-west jungle🔥 Inferno Edge (legendary weapon)+40 attack, +20% attack speed
South-east jungle💠 Aegis of Ether (legendary armor)+520 max HP, 15% damage reduction
  • Legendaries auto-equip when any hero walks over them — first come, first served.
  • When the carrying hero dies, the legendary drops where they fell — the enemy hero can steal it. High risk, high reward.
  • Bosses respawn ~150s after being slain. Boss kills also grant big gold and XP.

❤️ Combat, Healing & Respawns

  • Fountain: a glowing safe pad behind each town hall — heroes spawn and respawn there and heal back to full in seconds (14% HP/s). T recalls you home from anywhere.
  • Town-hall healing: standing near any friendly town hall or expansion base regenerates 70 HP + 25 mana per second — retreat to any base, not just home.
  • Map paths: rock ridges divide the jungle and seal the outer border — there are distinct lanes and jungle gaps, and you can't sneak around towers along the map edge. Towers hit hard (85 dmg, range 21): walking through one costs at least a couple of hits.
  • Pet heal: F — your companion restores 35% of your HP (25s cooldown).
  • Respawn: hero death = timer (6s + 2s per level) shown on your portrait, then you respawn at your base. Carried legendaries drop on death.
  • Damage numbers: orange = super effective, grey = resisted, white = normal.
